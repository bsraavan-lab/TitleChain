# TitleChain — Launch Video Narration

*Voice-over script · ~3 minutes · continuous narration*

---

Somewhere in Tamil Nadu, a family is about to make the biggest financial decision of their lives. They're buying a home. They've saved for years. They've walked the land, met the sellers, shaken hands. Now comes the paperwork — lawyers, banks, the registrar's office — and a growing stack of documents they're told will protect them.

At the heart of that stack sits one document: the Encumbrance Certificate. The EC is the property's memory. Every owner it has ever had. Every sale, every mortgage, every loan taken against it, every legal claim still attached to it. If anything can go wrong with a property, the warning is usually written somewhere in the EC.

And here is the problem: written in what? The EC is drafted in dense Tamil legal language — a vocabulary that even fluent Tamil speakers rarely encounter. Buyers can't read it. Investors can't read it. NRIs buying a home in the town they grew up in can't read it. Bank officers and educated professionals struggle with it. So the most important facts — a mortgage that was never released, a break in the chain of ownership, a claim from decades ago — stay buried in pages of text almost no one can decode. People hand the document to intermediaries and simply trust what they're told. Verification takes weeks, costs money, and depends on human eyes that can miss a single fatal line. This isn't a translation problem. It's a knowledge problem — the information exists, but it isn't accessible to the person whose life depends on it.

What if anyone could understand a property document with complete confidence, regardless of the language it was written in? That's TitleChain — an AI-powered platform that transforms complex legal documents into clear, structured, trustworthy knowledge.

You upload your Encumbrance Certificate. In moments, TitleChain reads it — every table, every entry, every date. Legal terminology becomes plain language, with the meaning preserved, not simplified away. The ownership history is rebuilt into a timeline you can actually follow, person to person, year to year. Mortgages, liens, and encumbrances are identified and labeled. Gaps and risks — a missing link in the chain, a loan with no release, years the search never covered — are flagged automatically. Instead of forty pages of legal text, you see one clear dashboard. And when you have a question, you ask it in plain English — and every answer points back to the exact line on the original document. Nothing asserted, nothing hidden. Evidence you can see.

This is bigger than one certificate. It's about democratizing legal knowledge — removing language as a barrier, reducing risk, and giving every citizen the power to make property decisions with open eyes.

Because understanding your property shouldn't depend on the language it was written in. It should be a right, not a privilege.
