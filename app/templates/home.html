Measured on real certificates

  • 3 of 4 certificates in our sample could not support the search they were ordered for
  • ~60s to read one, for under ₹5 — against 2.5–3 hours with a highlighter
  • 5–10 yrs how late these gaps normally surface today: at resale or foreclosure

Why a clean certificate can prove nothing

  • ▲ You pick the years blind. The search window is chosen up front, before anyone knows what is in the record.
  • ▲ Entries point outside it. Each one names an earlier deed, often from a year you did not pay for.
  • ▲ Nothing flags the edge. "Nothing found" and "you searched the wrong years" look identical on the page.

An encumbrance certificate is a bank statement for a plot of land: every registered transaction on it, between two dates you chose in advance.

What you get back

  • ● What it leaves out. The years covered, the years missed, and every earlier document nobody has read.
  • ● One errand, pre-filled. Office, village, survey numbers and dates, ready to paste into TNREGINET.
  • ● A record, not a promise. One report of what ran, what could not, and what you noted — for the file and the bank.

Why you can rely on it

  • ✓ Nothing is guessed. Findings come from fixed checks in code, so the same certificate always answers the same.
  • ✓ Every finding shows its source. Click a line and the exact region of the scanned page appears beside it.
  • ✓ It admits what it could not read. An unreadable date stays blank and waits for you. Nothing quietly counts as a pass.

TitleChain shows you what a certificate does and does not evidence. It does not give an opinion on title. That judgement stays yours.
